What QuickBooks does, and what it costs to rebuild

Every feature in QuickBooks Online Plus and Advanced, what BunzBooks already covers, and a build estimate for the rest — separated into prototype cost and real-application cost, because they differ by roughly an order of magnitude.

Compiled 20 August 2026

Which subscription you have

The QuickBooks API does not expose your subscription tier, so I can't read it directly. What your book demonstrably has: class tracking, inventory with on-hand quantities, custom fields on sales forms, and two users. Every one of those is available at Plus.

The features that would prove Advanced — batch invoicing, custom user roles, workflow automation, revenue recognition, fixed-asset depreciation, Spreadsheet Sync — leave no trace I can detect from the outside.

Confirm it in ten seconds: gear icon → Account and Settings → Billing & Subscription. Tell me which it says and I'll mark the tables accordingly. Everything below is annotated per tier, so the plan holds either way.

Two things changed in QuickBooks during 2026 that affect this plan. Sales Orders are now native to Plus and Advanced, along with Item Receipt, Moving Average Cost valuation, and Sales Order → Purchase Order linking — which is your dropship workflow, built in. And as of August 2026, Workforce Elite payroll and Bill Pay Elite are bundled into Advanced at no extra cost. Both are reasons to check what you're already paying for before rebuilding it.

How to read the estimates

Two numbers per feature, because they measure different work:

  • Prototype — building it in BunzBooks: dummy data, a view, the interactions. Enough to argue with the design.
  • Production — building it for real: Rust domain types, SQLite schema and migration, QuickBooks API mapping, outbox integration for writes, tests, and the React UI.

Sizes are S M L XL. The token figures are output tokens and come from what this session actually spent — the Rust M0 foundations (4,279 lines and 128 tests) ran about 70k, the first BunzBooks build about 40k, and each feature added since has landed between 8k and 30k. Input tokens run several times higher and depend on how much of the codebase gets re-read each turn.

Treat these as order-of-magnitude, not quotes. They assume the design is settled before code starts — which is why DESIGN.md exists. Review cycles, changed requirements, and debugging against live QuickBooks responses are the variables, and any one of them can double a line. The pattern from this session is that deciding what to build costs less than a third of building it, and skipping that decision costs more than both.

What I'd actually build, and in what order

Rebuilding QuickBooks feature-for-feature is about 1.4M output tokens of production work and would produce a worse QuickBooks. The value is in the places QuickBooks fits you badly, plus the handful of things you touch every day.

1 — Finish the fast front-end ≈ 190k

Sales orders, credit memos, sales receipts, A/P aging, expenses, attachments. This is qbo-local: QuickBooks stays the book of record and you stop waiting on it. Highest return per token in the whole table, because it removes daily friction without any accounting risk.

2 — Manufacturing, which QuickBooks simply lacks ≈ 550k

Assemblies, build transactions, landed cost by board-foot, scrap and yield. There is no tier of QuickBooks Online that does these — not Plus, not Advanced. Every one is currently a spreadsheet or your head. This is the part where a custom system is not a preference but the only option.

3 — The ledger, if you still want it ≈ 700k+

Double-entry core, posting rules, cash-versus-accrual, full history import, three months of parallel reconciliation. The largest and riskiest block, and the one your own brief softened — "start off QuickBooks-like, then build what makes sense for me." I would not start it until the first two are earning their keep.

Before any of it: check what you already pay for. Sales orders, SO→PO linking, item receipts and moving-average cost all went native in 2026. If you are on Plus or Advanced, several rows above are features you own and aren't using — and using them costs nothing.
